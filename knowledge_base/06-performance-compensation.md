## 1. Career Levels

### 1.1 Level Framework
NorthStar uses a standardized career level framework across all departments:

| Level | Title Example (Engineering) | Title Example (Product) | Title Example (Sales) |
|-------|---------------------------|------------------------|----------------------|
| L3 | Software Engineer | Associate PM | Account Executive |
| L4 | Software Engineer II | Product Manager | Senior AE |
| L5 | Senior Software Engineer | Senior PM | Enterprise AE |
| L6 | Staff Software Engineer | Staff PM | Principal AE |
| L7 | Principal Engineer / Eng Director | Director of Product | Sales Director |
| L8 | VP of Engineering | VP of Product | VP of Sales |
| L9 | SVP | SVP | SVP |
| L10 | C-Suite | C-Suite | C-Suite |

### 1.2 Individual Contributor vs. Management
Employees at L5 and above may pursue either an Individual Contributor (IC) or Management track. Both tracks have equivalent compensation bands and are considered equal in organizational stature. Switching between tracks requires approval from the department VP and People team.

### 1.3 Promotion Eligibility
- Employees must have been in their current level for a minimum of 12 months before being eligible for promotion (exceptions for extraordinary performance require VP approval)
- Promotions from L6 to L7 and above require a promotion packet reviewed by the department VP and the People team
- All promotions are effective at the beginning of a quarter (January 1, April 1, July 1, or October 1)

## 2. Compensation Structure

### 2.1 Base Salary
- Salaries are benchmarked annually against market data using Radford, Pave, and Levels.fyi data
- Target pay positioning: 60th percentile of market for the role, level, and geography
- Salary bands have a minimum, midpoint, and maximum. Employees cannot exceed the maximum of their band without a promotion

### 2.2 Geographic Pay Zones
NorthStar uses three geographic pay zones that apply a modifier to the national base salary band:

| Zone | Modifier | Locations |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Zone 1 (High Cost) | 100% | San Francisco Bay Area, New York City, Seattle, Boston |
| Zone 2 (Mid Cost) | 90% | Austin, Denver, Los Angeles, Chicago, Washington DC, San Diego, Portland |
| Zone 3 (Lower Cost) | 80% | All other approved remote locations |

**Important:** If a remote employee moves to a different zone, their compensation will be adjusted to the new zone's modifier effective the first of the month following the move. Employees must notify the People team at least 30 days before relocating (see Employee Handbook HR-POL-001 Section 3.2 for approved remote work states).

### 2.3 Salary Review Cycle
- Annual salary reviews occur in March, effective April 1
- Merit increases are based on performance rating, position within the salary band, and market data
- Typical merit increase ranges:

| Performance Rating | Merit Increase Range |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| Exceptional | 8-12% |
| Exceeds Expectations | 5-8% |
| Meets Expectations | 2-5% |
| Needs Improvement | 0% |
| Below Expectations | 0% (may trigger PIP) |

- Employees promoted within the last 6 months receive a prorated merit increase
- Employees on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) are not eligible for merit increases

## 3. Variable Compensation

### 3.1 Annual Bonus
Full-time employees at L4 and above are eligible for an annual performance bonus:

| Level | Target Bonus (% of Base) |
|-------|------------------------|
| L4 | 5% |
| L5 | 10% |
| L6 | 15% |
| L7 | 20% |
| L8 | 25% |
| L9-L10 | 30-40% |

Bonus payout is determined by:
- Company performance (50% weighting): Based on annual revenue and profitability targets set by the Board
- Individual performance (50% weighting): Based on the employee's annual performance rating
- Bonus multipliers range from 0% to 150% of target

Bonuses are paid in Q1 of the following year (typically February). Employees must be active employees on the bonus payout date to receive their bonus. Employees who resign or are terminated before the payout date forfeit their bonus, except in states where law requires pro-rated payment.

### 3.2 Sales Compensation
Sales team members (Account Executives, Sales Engineers, SDRs) have separate variable compensation plans:
- Account Executives: 50/50 base/variable split at OTE (On-Target Earnings)
- Sales Engineers: 70/30 base/variable split
- SDRs: 70/30 base/variable split
- Commission plans are documented separately in individual Sales Compensation Plan agreements
- Accelerators apply above 100% quota attainment (typically 1.5x for 100-120%, 2x for 120%+)
- Commission is paid monthly in arrears

### 3.3 Spot Bonuses
Managers at L7 and above may award spot bonuses of up to $2,500 to recognize exceptional one-time contributions. Spot bonuses above $2,500 require VP approval. The maximum total spot bonus per employee per year is $10,000.

## 4. Equity

### 4.1 New Hire Grants
Full-time employees at L4 and above receive equity grants (stock options or RSUs depending on company stage) as part of their compensation package:

| Level | Typical New Hire Grant Range |
|-------|---------------------------|
| L4 | $15,000 - $30,000 |
| L5 | $30,000 - $75,000 |
| L6 | $75,000 - $150,000 |
| L7 | $150,000 - $300,000 |
| L8+ | Individually negotiated |

Grant values are based on the fair market value at the time of grant and the current 409A valuation.

### 4.2 Vesting Schedule
- Standard vesting: 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff
- After the 1-year cliff: Monthly vesting for the remaining 36 months
- Vesting begins on the employee's start date

### 4.3 Refresh Grants
- Annual refresh grants are considered during the March compensation review cycle
- Refresh grants are targeted at 25% of the new hire grant value for the employee's current level
- Refresh grants are based on performance rating, retention risk, and current unvested equity balance
- Employees rated "Needs Improvement" or below do not receive refresh grants
- Refresh grants follow the same 4-year vesting schedule with a 1-year cliff

### 4.4 Promotion Grants
Employees who are promoted receive an equity grant equal to the difference between their current unvested equity run-rate and the target for the new level. Promotion grants vest on the same 4-year schedule.

### 4.5 Equity Upon Termination
- Voluntary resignation: Vested stock options must be exercised within 90 days of termination
- Involuntary termination (without cause): Vested stock options must be exercised within 90 days
- Termination for cause: All unvested equity is forfeited immediately; vested options must be exercised within 30 days
- RSUs that have vested are the employee's property regardless of termination type

## 5. Performance Management

### 5.1 Annual Performance Review
- Reviews are conducted in January-February for the prior calendar year
- Managers write reviews and assign ratings; reviews are calibrated across departments
- Employees complete a self-assessment before the manager review
- Review discussions occur in February, and final ratings are communicated by March 1

### 5.2 Performance Ratings

| Rating | Definition | Typical Distribution |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|
| Exceptional | Consistently exceeds all expectations. Impact significantly beyond role scope. Role model. | ~5% |
| Exceeds Expectations | Frequently exceeds expectations. Strong impact. Ready for increased scope. | ~20% |
| Meets Expectations | Consistently meets all expectations for the role. Solid, reliable contributor. | ~60% |
| Needs Improvement | Meets some but not all expectations. Specific areas require improvement. | ~10% |
| Below Expectations | Does not meet the minimum expectations for the role. Immediate action required. | ~5% |

Note: Distribution percentages are guidelines, not mandatory quotas. Managers are encouraged to rate fairly based on actual performance.

### 5.3 Mid-Year Check-In
A mandatory mid-year check-in occurs in July. This is not a formal rating but a structured conversation between the employee and manager to:
- Review progress on goals set in the annual review
- Provide feedback and identify development areas
- Adjust goals if priorities have changed
- Surface any concerns or blockers early

### 5.4 Continuous Feedback
Managers are expected to provide regular feedback (at minimum monthly 1:1s). Significant performance concerns should not be raised for the first time during the annual review. The People team provides feedback frameworks and coaching resources for managers.

## 6. Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

### 6.1 When PIPs Are Initiated
A PIP may be initiated when an employee receives a "Needs Improvement" or "Below Expectations" rating, or when performance concerns arise outside the review cycle. PIPs are coordinated between the manager and People team.

### 6.2 PIP Structure
- Duration: Typically 30-60 days (60-90 days for complex role transitions)
- Includes: Specific, measurable performance goals; weekly check-in schedule; resources and support provided; clear success criteria
- The employee must acknowledge receipt of the PIP (acknowledgment is not agreement with the assessment)

### 6.3 PIP Outcomes
- **Successful completion:** Employee returns to regular performance management. A subsequent performance concern within 6 months may result in a new PIP or separation.
- **Unsuccessful completion:** Typically results in termination. Severance may be offered per the Benefits Guide (HR-BEN-001) Section 10.2.
- **Employee resignation during PIP:** The employee may resign at any time. Standard resignation procedures apply; no severance is provided.

## 7. Professional Development

### 7.1 Annual Budget
Each employee receives $2,500 per year for professional development (see Employee Handbook HR-POL-001 Section 5.3). Employees at Director level (L7) and above receive $5,000 per year.

### 7.2 Tuition Reimbursement
Full-time employees with at least 2 years of tenure may apply for tuition reimbursement for degree programs (undergraduate or graduate) related to their role:
- Maximum reimbursement: $10,000 per calendar year
- Requires pre-approval from VP and People team before enrollment
- Employee must maintain a B average or equivalent
- Employee must remain employed for 2 years after receiving tuition reimbursement or repay the amount on a prorated basis
- Tuition reimbursement is separate from the annual professional development budget

### 7.3 Internal Mobility
Employees who have been in their current role for at least 12 months may apply for internal openings. Internal candidates are given preference when qualifications are equal. Managers may not block an internal transfer but may request a transition period of up to 4 weeks.

### 7.4 Mentorship Program
NorthStar offers a formal mentorship program matching employees with senior leaders outside their reporting chain. Mentorship cycles run for 6 months with a recommended meeting cadence of twice per month. Participation is voluntary and open to all full-time employees.

## 8. Recognition Programs

### 8.1 Quarterly Awards
Each department selects quarterly award recipients recognized at the company all-hands meeting:
- **Impact Award:** For exceptional business impact. Includes a $500 gift and a trophy.
- **Innovation Award:** For creative problem-solving or process improvement. Includes a $500 gift.
- **Culture Award:** For exemplifying NorthStar values. Includes a $500 gift.

### 8.2 Peer Recognition
NorthStar uses Bonusly for peer-to-peer recognition. Each employee receives $50/month in Bonusly points to give to colleagues. Points can be redeemed for gift cards, donations, or company swag. Unused points expire monthly.

### 8.3 Service Milestones
Employees are recognized at the following service milestones:
- 1 year: Welcome to the team recognition at all-hands
- 3 years: $250 gift of choice
- 5 years: $500 gift + extra floating holiday
- 7 years: Sabbatical eligibility (see Leave Policy HR-LOA-001 Section 8)
- 10 years: $1,000 gift + 2 extra floating holidays + recognition at annual company event
